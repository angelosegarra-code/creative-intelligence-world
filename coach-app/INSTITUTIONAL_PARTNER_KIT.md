# Organization Studio — partner conversation kit
Status: fictional-data development prototype; not deployed. No outreach sent.

## Positioning
Help people develop the capacity to direct their own learning. CI World structures perspective-taking, self-chosen action, professional questions and private reflection. The participant, not the technology, makes the decision.

## First audiences
### Adult education and continuing education
Problem: learners may wait for instructions rather than develop independent judgment.
Discovery question: Where do adult learners struggle to choose their own next learning step?
Illustrative use: a learner explores a question, chooses a small experiment, optionally shares it with an advisor, and reflects privately.
### Coaching and advisory organizations
Problem: maintaining continuity between sessions without turning personal exploration into surveillance.
Discovery question: How do clients carry learning forward between sessions?
Illustrative use: a client shares a chosen goal and receives a question from their assigned coach, retaining control of their next action.

## Invitation — adult education
Subject: Exploring self-directed learning with your adult learners
I'm developing Creative Intelligence World, a structured learning environment designed to help people explore different perspectives, make their own decisions and learn from experience.
Organization Studio brings this process into an institutional setting while allowing learners to choose what they share with their advisor.
Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation and a fictional walkthrough to explore whether this approach addresses a learning challenge in your organization?

## Invitation — coaching organizations
Subject: Supporting client learning between coaching sessions
I'm developing Organization Studio within Creative Intelligence World to support the learning that happens between coaching conversations. Clients choose their own goals and next actions, decide what to share, and reflect privately. Coaches can offer questions without taking ownership of the client's decisions.
Would you be open to a 15-minute conversation to explore a fictional demonstration and tell me where this might fit your practice?

## Demonstration sequence (15 minutes)
0–3: Ask about the institution's current challenge; establish that this is a fictional evaluation.
3–5: Show the administrator, professional and participant views.
5–8: Explore Observer, Dreamer, Alchemist and Visionary as flexible perspectives.
8–12: Walk through Choose, Share, Question, Reflect, Adapt, Withdraw. Explain that withdrawal blocks future access, not previously seen information.
12–15: Ask about usefulness, missing safeguards and whether a fictional-data evaluation is worth pursuing.
